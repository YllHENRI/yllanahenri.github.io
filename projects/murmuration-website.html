Murmuration - Website redesign

Overview

Murmuration is a Greentech company that provides environmental data visualizations to help cities, organizations, and companies make data-driven decisions for a more sustainable future.

As the sole UX/UI Designer, I was responsible for rethinking the company’s entire digital identity: from brand guidelines to the complete redesign of its corporate website.

This case study focuses on the redesign of Murmuration’s main website, a key communication and conversion tool for the company.

Problem

The old website was confusing and outdated, leaving users unsure of what Murmuration does.

Solution

A full UX redesign focused on clarity, structure and storytelling. All aligned with Murmuration’s new brand identity.

Results

Users can now clearly explain Murmuration’s mission after browsing with strong engagement across 10,000+ sessions since launch.

Problem

The previous website was outdated both visually and structurally. It suffered from poor navigation, a lack of brand consistency, and unclear messaging about the company’s purpose. Users often got lost in complex modals and unrelated content, leading to a high bounce rate and low engagement.

Solution

I redesigned the entire website experience to align with Murmuration’s new brand identity and improve usability. The new design focuses on clarity, storytelling, and readability, while reinforcing the company’s dual identity: technology and environmental awareness.

The navigation was simplified with a clear side menu, a dark mode interface adapted to users’ working conditions, and structured storytelling to highlight services and impact.

Project goals

Before starting the redesign, I defined a series of clear goals to guide both the design and development processes. These objectives focused on improving the user experience, reinforcing Murmuration’s new brand identity, and ensuring the website better reflected the company’s mission and values.

  • Goal 1

    Simplify the navigation and information architecture for better usability.

  • Goal 2

    Modernize the visual design to align with the new Murmuration brand identity.

  • Goal 3

    Clarify Murmuration’s mission and services through a clear, engaging storytelling flow.

  • Goal 4

    Improve website performance and engagement by reducing bounce rate and increasing session duration.

Research

To build an effective redesign, I first needed to understand how users perceived Murmuration and interacted with its old website.

I conducted a UX audit and competitor analysis, uncovering structural issues, unclear messaging, and navigation that often left users lost in endless modals. Using Google Analytics, I analyzed traffic, session duration, and behavior patterns to map where and how users were dropping off.

Below are the three main pain points identified during this research phase:

Finding 1

Users struggled to understand what Murmuration actually did.

Most visitors arrived on the website without a clear understanding of the company’s purpose. The content used technical vocabulary and lacked simple explanations or visual cues to clarify what Murmuration offered. As a result, users couldn’t easily identify the company’s services or its added value in the environmental data field.

This confusion often led users to leave the site prematurely, unsure of whether it matched their needs or interests.

Finding 2

The navigation was confusing and inconsistent.

The old website relied heavily on modal windows, some of which opened inside other modals. This nested structure made it difficult for users to retrace their steps or explore freely.

Important information such as services, project examples, or contact details were buried deep within the site and often went unnoticed.

Users described the browsing experience as “disorienting,” and several testers said they were unsure when they had reached the end of a page or section.

Finding 3

Environmental indicators lacked explanation and context.

One of Murmuration’s key strengths, its environmental data visualizations, wasn’t clearly explained.

Users saw the indicators but didn’t understand what they represented or how they connected to Murmuration’s mission.

Without a clear narrative or legend, these visuals failed to communicate their impact, leaving users confused about how Murmuration’s technology actually contributed to environmental insight.

Ideation & structure

With these insights, I began reshaping the website’s structure to focus on clarity, hierarchy, and storytelling.

I created several personas to represent the website’s core audiences, from institutional partners to environmental professionals and designed user flows that reflected their needs and goals.

A new sitemap was then built, drastically simplifying navigation and ensuring that each section of the site had a clear purpose.

Personas

To create a website that truly reflected Murmuration’s mission and connected with its audience, I developed two proto-personas from user testing results, website analytics, and contextual research. These proto-personas helped me better understand who our visitors were, what motivated them, and what obstacles they faced when exploring Murmuration’s digital platforms.

Claire, an environmental project manager, embodies the public-sector user: pragmatic, empathetic, and seeking clear, accessible insights to guide local sustainability decisions.

Marc, an operations director in the tourism sector, represents private-sector users driven by both sustainability and performance. He values visual, data-driven narratives that support strategic and economic choices.

These personas played a key role throughout the design process. They guided the structure of the navigation, the tone of the content, and the level of technical detail, ensuring every design decision remained user-centered and meaningful.

First round of User testing and team feedback

Goal: Validate navigation, comprehension, and task flow.

I conducted a first round of usability tests using the low-fidelity prototypes once the content was fully written. Participants were asked to perform simple tasks such as identifying Murmuration’s mission, finding services, or locating contact information.

Finding 1

Terminology remained unclear for non-expert users

Users liked the simplified structure, but many still struggled with certain technical terms used throughout the website.

Words related to environmental indicators or services weren’t always intuitive, which created small moments of confusion during navigation.

This made some users hesitate before clicking or exploring further.

Finding 2

Important content lacked visibility

Testers often mentioned that key information such as Murmuration’s mission or the value of its services was not prominent enough.

They expected essential explanations earlier in the journey, especially on entry pages.

As a result, some users felt uncertain about where to start or what to focus on first.

Finding 3

The homepage needed a stronger narrative

While the layout felt clearer, many users still couldn’t easily explain what Murmuration did after viewing the homepage.

They wanted a more guided introduction that connected the company’s mission, services, and impact in a simple way.

Without this storytelling layer, some users struggled to understand the overall purpose of the website.

Iteration phase

The Indicators page went through several rounds of iteration, as testers initially struggled to understand the meaning and relevance of Murmuration’s environmental indicators.

I explored multiple layout proposals to clarify the hierarchy, improve the explanations, and make the visuals easier to interpret.

These iterations focused on:

  • Highlighting key indicators
  • Adding clearer descriptions
  • Reorganizing sections for better readability
  • Strengthening the connection between data and real-world impact

During the redesign, the need for a more intuitive and impactful structure emerged.

To improve navigation clarity and support a smoother user journey, I decided to introduce a side menu.

This choice also aligned the corporate website with Murmuration’s new dashboard ecosystem, creating visual and functional consistency for users familiar with the product.

Final Design

Bringing the New visual identity Into the Interface

Once the structure was validated, I moved to high-fidelity mockups.

The goal was to apply Murmuration’s new visual identity: dark mode, gradients, and neon accents while keeping the interface clear and accessible.

This helped create a consistent experience across both the website and Murmuration’s dashboards.

Strengthening Visual Hierarchy and Storytelling

At this stage, I focused on refining the hierarchy, spacing, and tone of each page.

Key sections such as the homepage, services, and indicators received stronger visual cues and clearer narrative blocks to help users quickly understand Murmuration’s mission and value.

Ensuring Clarity, comfort and Readability

To ensure readability and visual comfort, I paid particular attention to contrast, typography, and spacing throughout the high-fidelity design. The dark interface was carefully balanced with high-contrast text and accent colors to improve legibility in indoor and low-light environments while reducing visual fatigue. I established a clear typographic hierarchy to guide the eye through content and make scanning effortless, reinforcing key information without overwhelming the user. Finally, I increased and standardized spacing between sections, components, and text blocks to give the layout more clarity and breathing room, helping to reduce cognitive load and create a more comfortable and accessible browsing experience.

Final Usability Testing

Due to time and context constraints, I wasn’t able to organize large-scale usability testing with full external users. Instead, I conducted a final round of tests with 9 participants, including coworkers and volunteers from the Athome community at La Cité. While not fully representative, this approach still allowed me to validate key assumptions and gather qualitative feedback on the final version of the website.

Testing goals

Understanding of Murmuration’s mission and services

Ensure that users quickly understand what Murmuration does, what it offers and the value of its environmental data solutions after navigating the website.

Navigation fluidity and user experience

Validate that users can move through the website smoothly and intuitively with a clear sense of orientation and comfort while exploring key sections.

Perception of the interface and visual identity

Assess how users perceive the overall design, visual coherence and brand identity and whether the interface feels engaging, professional and distinctive.

Key Outcomes

All participants were able to clearly explain what Murmuration does and what services it offers after navigating the website.

Navigation was described as fluid and intuitive, with particular appreciation for the “Indicators” and “Services” pages.

The interface and visual identity were perceived as coherent, distinctive and professional, reinforcing Murmuration’s brand positioning.

Several users also mentioned a sense of visual enjoyment, especially when interacting with the special pages of “Indicators” and “Services”, highlighting the impact of the new design direction.

Limitations and Areas for Improvement

This testing phase also helped identify opportunities for further refinement as some participants suggested:

  • Increasing spacing in certain sections to improve readability.
  • Strengthening storytelling to guide users more progressively through content.
  • Improving consistency across icons and visual cues.
  • Adding more subtle visual guidance to help users navigate complex information.

These insights highlighted clear opportunities for future iterations, reinforcing the idea that the website can continue to evolve and improve over time.

Results & Impact

The redesign of Murmuration’s website had a clear and positive impact on both user understanding and engagement.

By simplifying the structure, strengthening storytelling, and aligning the interface with the new visual identity, the website became a more effective communication and discovery tool.

User Impact

  • Users were able to clearly explain Murmuration’s mission and services after navigating the website.
  • Navigation felt fluid and intuitive, with particular appreciation for the Indicators and Services sections.
  • The interface was perceived as professional, coherent, and visually engaging, reinforcing trust in the brand.

These results confirmed that the redesign successfully reduced confusion and improved overall comprehension.

Learnings & Takeaways

Growing as a Designer through Independence

This project was my first real experience at Murmuration and also my first time working as the sole designer within a small team. Without a tutor or senior designer to guide me, I had to make decisions independently, find answers on my own, and learn directly from real constraints. While this was sometimes challenging, it became one of the most formative experiences of my journey, helping me gain confidence, autonomy, and professional maturity.

I learned that in real-world projects, UX theory must often be adapted to the context, taking into account business goals, technical limitations, budget, and stakeholder expectations. This experience taught me how to balance user-centered design with practical realities.

Challenges faced

Being the only designer was the biggest challenge. I had to navigate uncertainty, justify my design decisions, and sometimes compromise when UX principles conflicted with business or communication goals.

Another key challenge was making technical, data-oriented content understandable to non-expert users. Thanks to close collaboration with engineers, I learned how to translate complex environmental data into clearer, more accessible experiences.

This project also taught me a valuable lesson about collaboration: explaining UX choices clearly, listening to feedback, and finding common ground is an essential part of working in a company environment.

What I’m most proud of

What made this project particularly rewarding was the positive feedback from usability testing. Hearing that users found the website readable, intuitive, and even enjoyable to explore reinforced the value of small UX decisions such as spacing, hierarchy, and clarity.

I’m also very proud of the collaboration with the developer. We worked closely throughout the project, communicated constantly, and solved problems together. This collaboration helped me better understand technical constraints, contributed to smoother implementation, and even led me to participate in maintenance and technical adjustments after launch.

What I would do differently

With hindsight, I would dedicate even more time to UX thinking before interface design. While visual design and UX go hand in hand, I would focus more on fewer, more deeply thought-through design solutions rather than multiple visual variations.

Accessibility guided contrast and readability throughout this project, but with hindsight, I’d want to push it further by integrating things like screen-reader support and keyboard navigation earlier in the process, not just visual clarity.
